# 0002. Non-commercial for good

Status: accepted, 5 November 2026. Royce ruled it while the project was being scoped. See decision 5 of `plans/open-sg-law-brief.md`.

Context. The audience is lawyers first, and lawyers pay for LawNet, so a paid tier is the obvious way to fund the project. The Judiciary's terms (section 6) and the Strata Titles Boards' terms bar reproducing their content "for any commercial purposes whatsoever" without written permission, and say nothing about non-commercial reuse. The letter to the Supreme Court (ADR 0001) asks for non-commercial publication.

Decision. The platform has no paid tier, no ads, no sponsored placement and no paid API, and never will. The letters to the Supreme Court and AGC say so. The code is MIT, so anyone may build a commercial product from it, but under their own permissions, not ours.

Why. A permission given for a non-commercial site is void the day the site starts charging, and the Judiciary's terms are clear on that point. Stating it in the letter makes a yes more likely and gives the reader of the reply no room for doubt. Writing it down as permanent stops a later funding idea from quietly breaking the permission that Release B rests on.

Consequences.

- Hosting is paid out of pocket or by donation that buys nothing. Running cost stays a design constraint, as it is for openhansard.sg.
- A future change of mind needs a new letter to every owner whose terms bar commercial use, before any charge is introduced.
- Sources whose terms bar non-commercial reuse too (PDPC, IPOS, CCS, LawNet) are unaffected by this decision. They stay in the backlog until their owners say yes.

Alternatives.

- A free site with a paid tier for lawyers (bulk export, alerts). Rejected, because it is commercial use under the Judiciary's terms.
- Leave the question open until funding is needed. Rejected, because the letters must say one way or the other.
